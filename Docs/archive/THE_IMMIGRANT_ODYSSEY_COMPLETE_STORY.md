## DAY 2 (Tuesday): The University Registrar & The Catch-22
`[STATUS: Day 2/28 | Cash: €20.00 | Legal Hours: 0.0/20h | Dossier: 0/4 | Freshness: 70%]`

At 08:30 AM, you walk through the brick courtyards of the Universität zu Lübeck. Red oak leaves stick to the wet flagstones. 

You find Room 102: *Akademisches Auslandsamt (International Admissions)*. Behind a tall counter with an acrylic partition sits **Frau Rita Schneider**, 54, wearing a knitted emerald cardigan, her reading glasses hanging from a silver beaded cord around her neck. On her desk is a rotating wooden carousel of rubber stamps (*Holzstempel*), stacked folders, and a porcelain mug of chamomile tea.

"Guten Morgen," you say carefully, practicing the greeting you rehearsed on the bus.

Rita looks up. Her expression softens behind her glasses. "Guten Morgen. Passport and admission letter, please."

She inspects your conditional admission for Biomedical Engineering. "Exemplary marks from your home university. Truly impressive. But where is your receipt for the **€250.00 Semesterbeitrag** (Semester Administrative Fee)?"

"I have the money," you explain, leaning forward. "My family took out a loan to deposit €11,208 into an official Sperrkonto (Blocked Account) at Sparkasse Bank. But the bank will not disburse my monthly funds until I show them an official city address registration (*Meldebescheinigung*)."

Rita winces with genuine maternal sorrow. She places both hands flat on her desk.

"Ach, the infamous German Catch-22," Rita sighs. "Listen to the machinery:
1. You cannot unlock your bank account without an address registration from the Bürgeramt.
2. The Bürgeramt will not register your address without a signed lease (*Wohnungsgeberbestätigung*) from a landlord.
3. No landlord in Lübeck will give you a lease without proof of income or a bank statement.
4. And I cannot give you your student transit ticket and enrollment certificate without the **€250.00 fee**—which is locked in that very bank!"

"Then what do I do?" your voice cracks slightly. "My visa expires in 27 days."

Rita reaches across the acrylic barrier and gently taps your wrist.  
"My daughter went to study in Tokyo ten years ago. She cried on the phone because Japanese banks wouldn't give her a card. I know this terror. You are going to work. Kruma Express—the grocery dark store at North Bridge—is hiring bicycle couriers. They pay **€13.50 per hour**. Under the law, you can work up to 20 hours a week without losing your student status.  
Earn the €250. Rent a room with Caretaker **Hans Lokker** at Aegidienstraße 14—he is strict, but he is honest. Bring me that €250, and I will personally stamp your enrollment certificate. Now go, before the dark store morning shift closes."
